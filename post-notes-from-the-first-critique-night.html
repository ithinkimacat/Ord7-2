Notes from the first critique night

Six people, three unfinished pieces, and a rule against saying “I like it.” What actually happened when we tried to talk about work without scoring it.

We had a rule: no verdicts in the first ten minutes. Ask a question, describe what you see, or say nothing. It turns out that “I like it” is often a way of not looking closely enough to say anything else.

The first piece was a painting that had been reworked four times. The painter wanted to know whether the figure in the doorway was legible. Nobody in the room had noticed a figure at all. That is not a failure of the painting; it is a fact about the painting that the painter could not see from inside it.

The useful sentence

The sentence that changed the evening was: “I keep looking at the wrong corner.” Not a judgement, not a fix — a report from a pair of eyes that were not the maker’s.

We are doing it again in three days. Bring something unfinished. The point is not to be finished.
